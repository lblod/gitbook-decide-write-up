## Organisational Form and Governance Authority

For all information on this topic we kept in mind that the maturity level of the data space that we might want to establish is still early stage. Keeping in mind the levels of maturity within GAIA-X (initial, building, operational) we are somewhere between initial and building. That means that a lot of decisions will have to be made once we are more advanced. The DECIDE project was planned to be a pilot and should give the partners in the project enough information to collectively conclude on the future work that the partners would want to do to fully establish an operational data space for local decisions or decision-making.

The starting point for the information below can be found in the [DSSC toolbox](https://toolbox.dssc.eu/?pane=business\&business=organisational-form-and-governance-authority). If and where already possible this theory has been specified for the local decisions data space that is the goal of the DECIDE-project.

Possible scenarios then might be:

2. We evaluate the effort as valuable but don’t know yet if a permanent data space should be put in place. In this scenario we will continue to management of the data space as mentioned below.

Already looking ahead the information stated below is with scenario number 2 in mind.

### Purpose & Scope

The core purpose of a data space for local decisions and local decision-making is sharing information on or based on local decisions.

Possible use cases might be:

* finding information within the local government
* finding inspirations in other local governments
* evaluating local decisions in their relation to (central) government policies (e.g. SDGs) or maybe even corporate policies
* Reusing of the data by different actors such as ngo’s, consultants, ...
* controlling local governments (like the Agency for Home Affairs does in Flanders)
* reuse of the data in data space for reasons of accountability
* …

All reuse and enrichment on local decision data is valuable for the data space.

At this point of the life cycle of the data space we limit the kind of data we take into scope. These might be coming into scope as the data space proceeds and grows.

* You can only bring in data which actually has been decided on, not only preparational data. If we were to evolve on this it should be clear if data is decided upon or preparational.
* We only add annotations like locations, governing bodies to the local decisions (the basics) (e.g. we don’t identify road signs).
* As a baseline statement, this data space does not share personal data (at this point in time) (GDPR)
* Local decisions can contain non-open data (e.g. personal data). It is the responsibility of each municipality to make sure no such data is being entered in the data space before the necessary measures have been taken. The open data might contain personal data, but only in cases when it’s mandatory by law to publish these data.
* The local decision data space should not contain data on decisions on public bodies (f.i. private companies)
* We don’t plan to expand the enrichment with extra AI annotated or generated data. We first want to evaluate more profoundly the value of the use cases we had within the project period.

The partners in the project consortium will have to evaluate whether the keep the services that have been set up for the project to meet the deliverables set in the proposal up and running, for what period of time, what agreements need to be made on those topics, … This might be the case for the dashboard realised within the project.

At this point the main focus of the data space will be the sharing of data (focus on data and data sharing governance and the organizational governance needed for that) and not yet the sharing of technical or other services. An expansion of that kind could also be a growth scenario. So focus will be on institutional and data sharing agreements.

#### Key stakeholders

The key stakeholders during project phase, and the upcoming period after the project phase, will be the project consortium partners. The roles from within the project phase will be continued until further notice.

In the future these might be extend with:

* Other municipalities: They are the main actors who can decide to provide the basic local decision information within the data space. The ownership on this data is only to be brought back to them.
* A representation of the European Data Space for Smart Communities (DS4SSCC) (depending on the role this body would like to have)
* Other regional and/or national governments
* A representation of data.europa.eu(The official portal for European data)

The data might be used and/or enriched by:

* Administration(s) (e.g. other governments, also other institutions, …)
* Politics (e.g. local councils)
* Civil Society (e.g. Data Journalists, NGOs, Citizens)
* Economy (e.g. IT-Provider, consultants, … all private companies - Private partners that would like to share enriched data on top of data on local decisions)
* Research organization
* …

These might also be potential members of the data space. The formalities to support such a growth of the data space, will still have to be established if this would be an aspiration.

A rule could be that all use of data from the data space which leads to enrichments has to be reinjected into the data space too. In that way the data space can grow and become extra relevant. It has to be clear that enrichments/manipulations are not actual formal basic decisions and it should be clear how/by who/what party/what technical solutions enrichments were brought into the data space.

### Governance Principles

All basic principles on interoperability, data spaces, … and obviously all legislation on data and AI should be taken into account. Below are some of the principles that are specifically important for the data space:

* Adhere to the ethical data sharing agreement (available in draft with the projectteam)
* FAIR: Findable, Accessible, Interoperabel, Reusable
* Data sovereignty (f.i. validate that it is real municipal data (integrity of the information)
* Added value by sharing decisions (local level, regional, national and supranational level)
* Cost sharing (for the infrastructure)
* Importance of openness, neutrality, transparency
* Reliable AI (AI Advisory Board)
* Trust and transparency (f.i. controllable and explainable AI (possibility to check and correct the annotations (work in progress)) (also see Public trust and transparency assessment)\[SK5]

There is a possibility that the cost sharing principle and the openness of the information might conflict. It will be important to take this into account when putting a business model for the data space in place, if any and if ever.

The data space puts value in the use of AI-models to enrich local decision data. Nontheless this should not have a negative influence on the data quality and data sovereignty. For that reason use of European models is being pushed. All members in the data space are expected to activally think about good practices about the conscious use of AI solutions (AI Act).

### Decision-Making

Decision making in the data space will be a trade-off between sovereignty and overal involvement on one side and transparant structures for the day to day management of the data space.

Key decisions on essential aspects of the dataspace (partners, architecture, cost sharing, data included in the data space, data sovereignty, data standards, ... ) need to be taken in consensus. These decisions need to be taken by the governance authority board of the DataSpace (data space owners) (at this moment: all participants in the dataspaces, also DS4SSCC and dataspace support center should be involved). These group needs to meet at least twice a year (in line with rotating market mastership). Within this group there will be setup a technical and a non-technical group to discuss the relevant topics in depth. The decision boards of the data space is responsible to resolve conflicts and take high level decisions. The governance authority board is responsible to resolve conflicts if the decision boards are not able to and take high level decisions.

Every decision on publishing data and on the accessibility of this data needs to be taken by the local government. The ownership of the data rests with the individual local governement (data sovereignty).

To administer the day to day decisions on the data space a president (**market master**) should be appointed. This SPOC is key spokesperson and contact point for the data space. In the early phases this can be a role that alternates between the partners every six months (period to be evaluated). Market masters should, in case of doubt, first check with the chairman of their partner organization. If necessary cases should be escalated to the decisions boards (depending on technical or non-technical) or even to the governance authority in critical situations.

**Decision on membership**: taken by market master in line with rules stipulated by governance board. Whoever has the presidency should be responsible to lead first contacts. Process for managing new memberships has to be finetuned within the first 2 years based on actual new membership requests.

**Who gives access**:

* Open data = no restriction
* Closed data = data owner

**Technical decisions**: the technical board decides on technical standards

**Other decisions**: the non-technical board

### Rules & Policies

If new members want to **join** the data space; they need to assign a technical as well as a non-technical spokesperson to join each comity. Each city joining need to formally mandate someone to join the governance authority level.

The adherence to the **rules will be checked** by the president (market master) and the governance authority board of the DataSpace (data space owners). Anybody who wants to join the dataspace needs adhere the rules of repeatable data plan. The use of the data standards defined in the repeatable dataplan is mandatory.

Correctness and trustworthiness of data in the **data** space will be guaranteed by the publication of the basic decision data by the local governments (on not some make pretend).

These **data owners** remain the main source of truth. Governance authority makes sure that this correct identification of the data owners/publishers is being verified. Data must adhere to all higher level national and european (GDPR etc.). The data owner is responsible for that. Openness and terms of use for the data are defined by the data owners.

If rules are violated, a **complaint can be made with the governance authority board** and they need to decide upon.

### Operating Model

Decision making in the data space will be a trade-off between sovereignty and overall involvement on one side and transparent structures for the day to day management of the data space.

As discussed above will key decisions be taken in consensus. These decisions need to be taken by the governance authority board of the DataSpace (data space owners) . These group needs to meet at least twice a year. Within this group there will be setup a technical and a non-technical group to discuss the relevant topics in depth. Complains also need to be treated by the entire board.

Every decision on publishing data and on the accessibility of this data needs to be taken by the data owners. The ownership of the data rests with the individual local governement (data sovereignty).

To administer the day to day decisions on the data space a president (market master) should be appointed. This SPOC is key spokesperson and contact point for the data space. In the early phases this can be a role that alternates between the partners every six months.
