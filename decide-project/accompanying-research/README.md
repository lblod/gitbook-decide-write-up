# Accompanying research

## Research-based learning for civil servants in Baden-Württemberg

In addition to the accompanying research carried out by the openDVA group at _Friedrich Schiller University_ in Jena, the _Kehl University of Applied Sciences_, under the academic leadership of _Prof. Dr.-Ing. Antje Dietrich_, Dean of Studies for the Bachelor’s degree programme in Digital Public Administration, also played a complementary role in the accompanying research as part of the DECIDe project.

Whilst the openDVA group built on previous collaborative projects such as Canarėno, simpLEX and KollOM-Fit, and in particular investigated how legal norms and local government decisions can be made FAIR-compliant and usable for data-driven decision support with the aid of knowledge graphs and Semantic Web technologies, the _Kehl University of Applied Sciences_ placed a particular emphasis on linking research, local government administrative practice and academic teaching.\[1] As a university with a specific educational mandate for the senior civil service (dt.: Gehobener Verwaltungsdienst) in Baden-Württemberg, _Kehl University of Applied Sciences_ has direct access to local government administrative practice. DECIDe thus offered the opportunity not only to examine current issues relating to local government data spaces, semantic interoperability, open government and data-driven administrative modernisation from a purely academic perspective, but also to trial their integration into the current training of future administrative professionals. To this end, the project was used simultaneously as a teaching and learning space for inquiry-based learning. In this context, inquiry-based learning refers to a higher education pedagogical approach in which students work through key phases of a research process – from formulating a research question and selecting appropriate methods to analysing, evaluating and reflecting on the results – using real-world problems. Within the context of DECIDe, current challenges relating to local government’s digital transformation could be directly linked to issues arising from administrative practice. At the same time, particular attention was paid to examining issues relating to data space architecture, semantic interoperability and evidence-based administrative modernisation.

As part of the training programme for senior civil servants in Baden-Württemberg, DECIDe was integrated as a model initiative into the Public Management and Digital Public Administration Management (DVM) degree programmes during the 16-month project. This resulted in a reciprocal transfer of knowledge: findings and questions arising from the research project were incorporated into teaching, whilst students’ analyses opened up new practical administrative perspectives on the data space and application scenarios addressed in the project. The accompanying academic research thus had a transfer effect beyond the immediate evaluation of the project, contributing to the training of future specialists and managers in public administration.

## Integration into degree programmes

### DECIDe seminar as part of the specialisation in _Information Management_ within the ‘_Public Management_’ Bachelor’s degree programme:

#### Student reflection from a local government perspective

As part of the Information Management specialisation, 25 students on the Bachelor’s degree programme in Public Management undertook a seminar during the 2025/2026 winter semester in which the EU project DECIDe and the _European Data Space for Smart Communities_ were discussed, initially with a focus on the role of externally funded projects in local authority digitalisation strategies.\[2] Working in groups, the students used SWOT analyses to develop a local authority perspective on the DECIDe project and produced term papers with a theoretical and empirical basis, with their recommendations for action serving both practical purposes and the further development of the project. The highlight of the seminar was a student excursion to Smart City Bamberg on 27 November 2025, which served to familiarise participants with and analyse smart city strategies, use case development and the municipal open-source platform ‘Civitas Core’ on site. Data walks and ‘data stories’ – a special form of lecture – enabled the participating students to gain concrete, hands-on experience of the applications of data spaces in urban planning, tourism and cultural outreach.\[3] The link between research, teaching and administrative practice was further strengthened through application-oriented learning arrangements with the pilot _City of Freiburg_. In cooperation with the _City of Freiburg_ and Ivan Acimovic – a long-standing lecturer at the _Kehl University of Applied Sciences for Public Administration_ and project lead for the ‘_Daten:Raum:Freiburg’_ smart city project – the data excellence strategy was presented in lecture sessions as an example of urban data spaces and contextualised within the framework of open government and smart cities. In collaborative learning groups, students examined the technical, organisational and governance issues surrounding a Legislative and Decision Data Space from the perspective of local authorities in a written assignment. This resulted in the following five assignments (each involving five students), which have not yet been published and which open up additional perspectives:

* _Shaping Europe’s green and digital future – the local significance of the European Data Space for smart communities, using DECIDE as an example_
* _AI chatbot for the Freiburg City Council information system: What is the potential of using an AI-supported chatbot in a local authority’s council information system – examined using the example of the City of Freiburg’s AI chatbot – and what factors determine its usefulness?_
* _What are the success factors for municipal data excellence? – Using the City of Freiburg as an example_
* _Requirements for setting up an urban data space, using the City of Bamberg as an example_
* _Developments in applications for an urban data space, using the example of the ‘Innovative Disaster Management’ use case in the city of Bamberg_

### DECIDe seminar as part of the ITIL and Distributed Systems sub-module in the ‘Digital Public Administration Management’ Bachelor’s degree programme: Active involvement of students in an empirical research process conducted by DECIDe

In the summer semester of 2026, the EU project DECIDe was once again integrated into the teaching programme at the _Kehl University of Applied Sciences for Public Administration_ in the form of a student seminar within the Bachelor’s degree programme in Digital Public Administration (DVM).\[4] As part of the _‘ITIL and Distributed Systems’_ sub-module in the DVM programme, eight sixth-semester students explored current challenges in the digitalisation of public administration. The focus was on the question of how data rooms, IT service management and user-centred digital administrative services can contribute to an efficient, transparent and needs-oriented public administration. DECIDe served not merely as a theoretical case study, but as the direct subject of research-based learning. The students gained insights into the design and practical testing of European data rooms and were able to reflect on their technical, organisational and user-related requirements using an ongoing European research project as a basis. In addition, on 15 June 2026, experiences from the DECIDe pilot &#x43;_&#x69;ty of Freiburg_ and its urban data room were incorporated into the course. Furthermore, the participation of the project partner _ui! Urban Innovation_ ensured that a private-sector perspective on the development of data-based solutions for public administration was taken into account.\[5] In this way, municipal, academic and private-sector perspectives were linked within a shared teaching and learning environment.

A particular focus of the seminar was on actively involving students in the preparation of the quantitative survey within the DECIDe project, which was scientifically coordinated by _Kehl University of Applied Sciences_ on behalf of the Belgian project lead. The aim of the survey was to systematically record the requirements, expectations and assessments of relevant stakeholders regarding the municipal application scenarios developed in the project.\[6] The main survey was carried out in July and August 2026 in the participating pilot cities. The survey instrument was developed during a project phase in which individual use cases were still being finalised. This placed particular demands on the research design: on the one hand, the questionnaire had to be prepared at an early stage; on the other hand, differing stages of development and municipal priorities had to be taken into account appropriately. The design was therefore based on the intended project outcomes as well as the requirements and application contexts of the participating pilot cities. In line with a stakeholder-oriented approach, perspectives from public administration, politics, business, academia and civil society were taken into account. To ensure methodological quality, the survey instrument was pre-tested before the main survey began. Pre-tests make it possible to identify potential comprehension difficulties, ambiguities in response options, problems with the flow of the questionnaire and other methodological weaknesses at an early stage. They thus form an important part of quality assurance for standardised surveys.

The students were directly involved in this process. Working in collaborative learning groups, they reviewed the questionnaire intended for the pilot cities of Freiburg and Bamberg from the perspective of potential users, identified weaknesses and developed specific recommendations for the further design of the survey instrument and the planned user tests. The results of the students’ pre-tests were subsequently analysed as part of the accompanying academic research and provided further insights into areas for optimisation prior to the main survey.

Involvement in a real-world pre-test thus generated mutual added value for both teaching and the research project. For DECIDe, this provided an additional quality assurance perspective on the survey instrument. At the same time, the students were able to familiarise themselves with empirical research methods not merely in theory, but to directly understand their application and significance within an ongoing European research project. In particular, skills in the areas of questionnaire evaluation, user research, user experience, methodological quality assurance and user-centred digitalisation of public administration were strengthened.

The seminar thus serves as a prime example of Kehl University of Applied Sciences’ approach to combining academic accompanying research with research-based learning. The students were not merely informed about the results of a research project, but were actively involved in a concrete research process. At the same time, an additional user perspective was incorporated into the preparation of the empirical survey. DECIDe thus served both as a subject of research and as a practice-oriented learning environment for future professionals in digital public administration.

### Student dissertation in the Master’s programme ‘European Public Administration Management’: DECIDe as the subject of an independent academic study

The integration of DECIDe into research and teaching at the Kehl University of Public Administration continued at Master’s level as well. Within the Master’s programme in European Public Administration, the EU research project formed the subject of a student’s final dissertation under the academic supervision of Prof. Dr.-Ing. Antje Dietrich.\[7] DECIDe thus served not only as a subject for teaching and learning, but also as the empirical focus of an independent academic thesis. The thesis centres on the question of how local government decision-making data can be made available and utilised as Linked Open Government Data, and what specific European added value this data provides in the context of European data spaces. The EU research project DECIDe serves as a case study for a ‘Legislative and Decision Data Space’, which semantically links local decisions and legal acts and is intended to make them accessible across Europe via standardised interfaces. Methodologically, the thesis follows a qualitative research approach. The student conducted expert interviews with key DECIDe project partners – including the project management team, representatives of the pilot cities and stakeholders from the European Data Space for Smart Communities (DS4SSCC) – in order to gather different perspectives on data spaces for local government decisions. The focus is on three sets of questions: (1) how municipal decision-making data can be made available, both technically and organisationally, and utilised in practice; (2) what added value European cooperation offers from a municipal perspective (e.g. through transferable standards, shared infrastructure and scalable use cases); and (3) what implications this has for the design of European data spaces. The interviews will be analysed exclusively for academic purposes; respondents may be anonymised upon request. This Master’s thesis thus expands the academic discourse on DECIDe by contributing an independent qualitative study of the project’s European dimension. Whilst the project’s operational activities focus in particular on the development and testing of specific technical and local authority application scenarios, this thesis enables a complementary reflection on the conditions under which local authority decision-making data can be made interoperable and reusable beyond local and national contexts.

### Capacity building and further development of teaching and continuing professional development at Kehl University of Applied Sciences

The integration of the DECIDe project into seminars, empirical research activities, students’ final dissertations and field trips enabled the Kehl University of Public Administration to forge links between research, academic teaching and municipal administrative practice. Students were able to address current issues relating to data spaces, data governance, semantic interoperability, open government and user-centred digitalisation of public administration using specific local government case studies, thereby applying academic methods to real-world challenges in administrative practice.

The various teaching and research formats facilitated a reciprocal transfer of knowledge. Findings and issues identified through DECIDe, as well as the experiences of the participating pilot cities, were directly incorporated into academic teaching. At the same time, student analyses, pre-tests and academic papers provided additional perspectives on issues relating to data governance, project organisation, use-case development and the user-centred design of digital administrative services. Insofar as these findings were incorporated into the further design of the project or its applications, feedback loops were created between scholarly reflection, academic training and local government innovation practice.

The experiences gained from DECIDe particularly highlight the potential of research-based learning as a transfer tool at a university for the public sector. Through active involvement in real-world research and development processes, students were not only able to acquire specialist knowledge but also to further develop methodological, analytical and interdisciplinary skills in dealing with complex digitalisation and transformation processes within public administration. This is particularly significant against the backdrop of growing demands for a data-driven, interoperable and collaborative public sector.

Beyond the direct skills development of the participating students, DECIDe also generated an institutional impetus for learning and development at Kehl University of Applied Sciences. The exemplary integration of an ongoing European research project into various degree programmes and qualification levels provided practical insights into how current research and innovation topics can be systematically linked to the training of future specialists and managers in public administration. The accompanying academic research thus had an impact not only on the DECIDe project itself, but also on the further development of research-based teaching and learning formats at Kehl University of Applied Sciences.

The insights gained during the project’s duration form a basis for incorporating topics such as municipal data rooms, data governance, interoperability, data-driven administrative modernisation and user-centred digital administrative services more extensively into the university’s teaching and continuing professional development programmes in future. The teaching and learning formats tested in DECIDe can serve as a starting point for longer-term curricular and pedagogical development at the university.

In this way, the accompanying academic research has an impact that extends beyond the immediate duration of the research project: findings from a European innovation project are being harnessed for academic qualification, linked to experience from local government practice, and, in the long term, made available for the training and further education of senior civil servants in Baden-Württemberg. DECIDe thus contributes to the sustainable transfer of expertise and knowledge between European research, higher education and local government practice.
